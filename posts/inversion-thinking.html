Inversion: The Mental Model That Solves Problems Backwards

February 2026 · 6 min read

Charlie Munger once said, "Invert, always invert." It's a deceptively simple idea that might be the most powerful mental model you'll ever learn. Instead of asking how to achieve success, ask: how would I guarantee failure? Then do the opposite.

The Origin: Carl Jacobi's Insight

The concept comes from the 19th-century mathematician Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi, who advised solving difficult problems by inverting them. Munger adopted this into his decision-making toolkit and applied it to investing, business, and life with extraordinary results.

The genius of inversion is that it's often easier to identify what leads to failure than what leads to success. We have strong intuitions about what goes wrong - we just rarely use them proactively.

How Inversion Works in Practice

The process is straightforward:

  1. Define your goal. What do you want to achieve?
  2. Invert it. Ask: what would guarantee failure? What should I absolutely avoid?
  3. List the failure modes. Be specific and comprehensive.
  4. Create a plan that avoids those failures. Your avoidance list becomes your strategy.

Example: Building a Successful Investment Portfolio

Instead of asking "How do I build wealth through investing?", ask "How would I guarantee I destroy my portfolio?"

The answers come quickly:

  • Trade constantly based on emotions and headlines
  • Use excessive leverage
  • Concentrate everything in a single speculative bet
  • Ignore valuation and buy whatever is popular
  • Never study the businesses I invest in
  • Panic sell during market downturns

Now invert each item, and you have a solid investment framework: be patient, avoid leverage, diversify sensibly, focus on value, do your research, and stay calm during volatility. You've arrived at sound investment principles without needing to be a genius - just by systematically avoiding stupidity.

Example: Career Decisions

Asking "How do I have a great career?" is vague. But "How would I guarantee career failure?" produces concrete answers:

  • Never develop new skills
  • Be unreliable and miss deadlines
  • Burn bridges with every colleague
  • Refuse to adapt to change
  • Stop learning after formal education ends

Avoiding these failure modes doesn't guarantee spectacular success, but it puts you ahead of most people. As Munger puts it: "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent."

Why Inversion Is So Powerful

It bypasses blind spots. We're naturally optimistic about our plans. Inversion forces us to confront what could go wrong before it does.

It simplifies complex problems. "How do I succeed?" has infinite answers. "How do I fail?" has a manageable list. Working with the smaller set is more actionable.

It pairs well with other mental models. Use inversion alongside pre-mortem analysis, second-order thinking, and margin of safety for a robust decision-making framework.

Start Using Inversion Today

Pick any decision you're currently facing. Write down the goal. Then ask: "What would make this fail spectacularly?" List everything. Then build your plan around avoiding those items.

You'll be surprised how much clarity this simple technique provides. Sometimes the best path forward is knowing exactly which paths to avoid.
